I teach accounting and valuation at the Yale School of Management, from Yale College undergraduates to MBA and doctoral students.

Yale SOM · ACC 270 & MGT 502 · Undergraduate & Graduate

Foundations of Accounting & Valuation

A comprehensive introduction to accounting and valuation: how to read, interpret, and analyze the balance sheet, income statement, and statement of cash flows, and how to apply valuation techniques such as discounted cash flow and risk/return analysis to make informed investment, credit, acquisition, and business decisions. The course is taught through interactive lectures and real-world cases built on corporate annual reports and financial statements.

I teach it to Yale College undergraduates (ACC 270) and to graduate students from across the university, including the Schools of Public Health and the Environment (MGT 502).

Yale SOM · MGT 816 · MBA Elective

The Private Firm CFO

Private firm finance is different: no market pricing, no analyst coverage, no mandated disclosure. This course examines the CFO role at private, entrepreneurial companies, following a company’s lifecycle from formation through exit — financial infrastructure, capitalization and equity compensation, driver-based forecasting, board reporting, revenue recognition, fundraising, and exits.

Students work through two cases and two problem sets, and leave with work they keep: a capitalization table from formation through Series A, an operating forecast built with AI tools, a board memo on equity compensation and its tax implications, and a critique of a board reporting package.

Yale SOM · MGMT 701 · PhD Seminar

Seminar in Accounting Research II: Empirical Papers

A doctoral seminar (co-taught with Aneesh Raghunandan) that surveys empirical financial accounting research and trains students to evaluate and produce it, through paper presentations, a replication exercise, and a full referee report. Topics span disclosure and its regulation, governance of the executive and the board, earnings management, analysts, AI/ML in financial reporting, ESG, and a dedicated session on labor and accounting — the intersection of labor economics and accounting that anchors much of my own work.

I also advise doctoral students at Yale SOM, currently as dissertation co-chair for Ping R. Gong, and teach a PhD module on using AI tools in empirical research, detailed on the AI & Research page.
